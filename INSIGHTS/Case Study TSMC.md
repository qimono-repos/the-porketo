## Executive takeaway

**Classification: positive fundamental evidence, but no confirmed intraday buy signal.**

TSMC's Q3 results and Q4 guidance support the view that demand for advanced AI semiconductors remains strong. That is constructive context for Nvidia, a major AI-chip designer and TSMC customer. However, TSMC's growth cannot be translated directly into Nvidia's future revenue or share-price performance. On October 15, both TSMC's U.S.-listed ADR (TSM) and Nvidia (NVDA) closed lower despite the strong TSMC report. The divergence between company fundamentals and share-price reaction is a reason to investigate market positioning, valuation and broader risk appetite, not proof that a rebound or further decline is imminent.

For an intraday strategy, do not open a position based on the earnings report alone. Refresh market data during the next regular session and require price action, volume and sector confirmation.

## 1. TSMC Q3 2026 earnings: what the report says

The uploaded earnings release reports:

- **Q3 revenue:** US$46.72 billion, up 41.2% year over year.
- **Net income:** NT$763.06 billion, up 68.7% year over year.
- **Gross margin:** 66.4%.
- **Net profit margin:** 51.1%.
- **Q4 2026 revenue guidance:** US$52.3 billion to US$54.3 billion.
- **Q4 gross-margin guidance:** 65% to 67%.
- **Q4 operating-margin guidance:** 56.5% to 58.5%.

Using the midpoint of Q4 revenue guidance (US$53.3 billion), the forecast implies approximately **14.1% sequential revenue growth** over Q3 revenue of US$46.72 billion. This is a calculation from the reported figures, not a company-issued growth percentage.

Net income grew faster than revenue. That indicates strong earnings conversion in the reported quarter, although the release alone does not isolate how much came from product mix, pricing, manufacturing efficiency, foreign exchange or operating leverage.

The report's process-technology mix shows 3nm at 33%, 5nm at 28%, 7nm at 10%, 2nm at 8%, and other technologies at 21% of wafer revenue. Thus, the specified 7nm-and-more-advanced categories account for 79% of wafer revenue. The report points to the commercial importance of advanced manufacturing; it does not, by itself, quantify the future profit contribution of the 2nm ramp.

## 2. What this implies for Nvidia and the AI semiconductor ecosystem

### FACTS supported by the source material

- TSMC reported rapid year-over-year revenue and earnings growth.
- Its Q4 guidance points to further sequential revenue expansion while maintaining high expected margins.
- Advanced manufacturing technologies account for a substantial share of wafer revenue.
- Nvidia is an important participant in the AI-chip ecosystem and a TSMC customer, as referenced in the Reuters reporting used in the prior analysis.

### INFERENCE, not a reported fact

These results support the manufacturing side of Nvidia's AI-growth thesis. Strong demand for TSMC's advanced processes is consistent with substantial demand for advanced silicon. But it does **not** prove Nvidia's future GPU shipment volumes, sales growth, gross margins, customer orders, or the return on investment earned by AI infrastructure buyers.

The economic transmission chain is:

1. Cloud providers and other customers invest in AI compute.
2. That investment can translate into demand for Nvidia accelerators and related systems.
3. Chip designers rely on manufacturing partners such as TSMC, whose capacity, yields and process ramps affect supply.
4. Delivered products and customer economics eventually show up in financial results.

Each link can weaken or delay the outcome. Manufacturing demand is useful corroborating evidence, not a substitute for Nvidia's own results, guidance and valuation.

The prior analysis referenced Reuters reporting that TSMC exceeded analyst expectations while semiconductor shares were under pressure, amid concerns including the sustainability and returns of AI infrastructure spending. The most reasonable interpretation is that investors may be separating evidence of current AI demand from the price they are willing to pay for future profits. Valuation, prior expectations, broader market weakness and risk appetite can outweigh a positive earnings surprise in a given session.

A falling stock after strong results does not by itself mean the growth thesis is broken. Equally, strong fundamentals do not guarantee a near-term price recovery.

## 5. Qimono Trading decision

**Conclusion: positive fundamental evidence; intraday entry not yet justified by this report alone.**

The TSMC report strengthens the case that demand for advanced AI semiconductors is substantial. Nvidia may benefit from this ecosystem, but its share price also reflects its own earnings outlook, valuation, supply constraints, customer economics, market-wide sentiment and expectations for AI spending returns.

The observed decline in both TSM and NVDA on October 15 is a counter-signal worth investigating. It does not establish whether the next move will be up or down. Since the U.S. regular session was closed at the time of the prior analysis, no verified executable quote for the next session had been established. The disciplined action was therefore to wait for fresh data rather than trade from a narrative.
