# Risks

## Purpose

Define the project's concept of **risk** as a durable governance concept.

A risk describes a possible future event, condition, or circumstance that could affect the project's objectives, operations, capital, rules, evidence, or ability to execute.

The project treats risk as a governance object that should be made visible, reasoned about, monitored, and learned from.

## Definition

A **risk** is a potential event or condition that, if it occurs, may produce an effect on an objective or on the ability of the system to operate as intended.

A risk is therefore about **possibility and potential effect**.

A deviation is about **a difference that has actually occurred** between expected and actual behavior.

The distinction is:

**Risk → may happen.**

**Deviation → did happen differently.**

A risk may later result in a deviation, but the two concepts remain distinct.

## Minimum Risk Record

When a risk is material enough to become project knowledge, the journal or risk record should preserve:

1. **Risk** — the event or condition that may occur.
2. **Cause / Source** — why the risk could arise.
3. **Potential Effect** — what could happen if the risk materializes.
4. **Exposure** — the relevant dimension of potential impact, such as capital, percentage, quantity, timing, liquidity, execution, information, or operational continuity.
5. **Likelihood, when useful** — the current assessment of how plausible the risk appears.
6. **Controls / Mitigations** — actions or constraints intended to reduce likelihood or impact.
7. **Trigger / Indicator** — observable evidence that the risk is becoming active or has materialized.
8. **Response** — what should be done if the trigger occurs.
9. **Owner / Responsibility** — who or what is responsible for monitoring or acting on the risk.
10. **Status** — the current state of the risk.
11. **Outcome** — what actually happened if the risk materialized.
12. **Disposition** — the resulting treatment, such as Accepted, Mitigated, Transferred, Avoided, or Closed.

Not every risk requires every field at every stage. The record should contain enough information to make the risk understandable and actionable.

## Risk Is Not Automatically a Problem

The existence of a risk does not mean that an error has occurred.

A risk may be:

- accepted deliberately;
- reduced through a control;
- avoided by changing the plan;
- transferred to another mechanism or party;
- monitored without immediate intervention; or
- eventually shown to have been irrelevant or no longer applicable.

The purpose of recording a risk is to make the uncertainty explicit before it becomes an event.

## Relationship to Deviations

Risks and deviations form a useful temporal pair.

**Risk:** a possible departure from expected behavior.

**Deviation:** an observed departure from expected behavior.

If a risk materializes and produces a difference between planned and actual behavior, the resulting event should be recorded as a deviation as well.

The relationship can therefore be:

**Risk → Trigger → Event → Deviation, when actual behavior differs from expectation → Response → Outcome → Learning.**

A risk does not need to become a deviation. Preventive controls may prevent the event entirely.

## Relationship to Exceptions

An exception is an intentional departure from an established rule.

A risk may justify preparing an exception path in advance, but the existence of a risk does not itself authorize an exception.

When an exception is actually used, the journal records the concrete deviation and its rationale.

## Relationship to Precedent

Historical risk cases can become precedent.

When a new risk resembles a previously documented case, the AI should identify the **single best precedent** and use its reasoning as relevant context.

A precedent does not expire merely because time passes.

A later case may distinguish or explicitly overrule a precedent, but the historical case remains preserved.

## Risk Lifecycle

The project treats risk as a lifecycle rather than a one-time label:

**Identify → Analyze → Decide treatment → Monitor → Trigger or expire → Respond → Record outcome → Learn.**

A risk should not remain indefinitely active merely because it was once identified.

Its status should evolve as evidence changes.

## Risk and Rule Evolution

A risk may reveal that an existing rule is incomplete, too strict, too permissive, or missing a necessary condition.

When that happens, the rule should not be silently rewritten.

The project should preserve:

1. the original rule;
2. the risk that exposed the limitation;
3. the observed evidence;
4. the rationale for the change; and
5. the new rule or control.

This preserves visible evolution of the project's operating jurisprudence.

## Risk vs. Deviation

| Concept | Core question | Time orientation |
|---|---|---|
| Risk | What might happen? | Future / uncertain |
| Deviation | What happened differently? | Observed / actual |
| Exception | Why was the rule intentionally not followed? | Decision / action |
| Precedent | What prior case can inform this case? | Historical / reusable |

These concepts may appear in the same case, but they should not be collapsed into one another.

## Trading-Specific Risk Dimensions

The project should be able to recognize risks involving, among other things:

- **Market risk** — price movement affecting an intended action or position.
- **Liquidity risk** — insufficient available liquidity to execute the intended movement.
- **Execution risk** — the broker or exchange does not execute the intended movement as expected.
- **Timing risk** — a condition changes before the planned action can occur.
- **Operational risk** — connectivity, tooling, human availability, automation, or process failure interferes with execution.
- **Data risk** — stale, incomplete, incorrect, or inconsistent information affects a decision.
- **Accounting risk** — source events, positions, strategy lots, or derived values become inconsistent.
- **Rule risk** — an existing rule produces an unintended or incomplete behavior under a new circumstance.
- **Model / thesis risk** — the assumptions supporting a strategy or investment prove incorrect.
- **Concentration risk** — exposure becomes overly dependent on one asset, ecosystem, mechanism, or assumption.
- **Governance risk** — the system cannot clearly explain why a decision, exception, or rule change occurred.

These are categories, not automatic judgments. A specific risk should be recorded in terms of its concrete cause, potential effect, and evidence.

## Project Principle

> A risk is a documented possibility whose potential effect is important enough to make visible before it becomes an event.

The objective is not to eliminate uncertainty.

The objective is to make uncertainty **visible, explainable, monitorable, and useful for decision-making and continual improvement**.
