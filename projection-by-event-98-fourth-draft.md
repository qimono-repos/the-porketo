# Projection by Event 98 — Fourth Draft: The Three-Team Performance & Resource Allocation Model

**Status:** Conceptual planning draft. This is a proposed operating model, not an execution record or a final allocation schedule.

**Planning horizon:** Event 98 / Checkpoint 98, Friday, October 30, 2026, 22:00 Halifax time.

## 1. Purpose

Evolve the $100 Trading Ticket into a dynamic 35-player squad. Each underlying asset is one player, whether exposure is held as crypto or as a tokenized stock.

The model assigns players to three teams with different allocations of invested capital, analytical attention, compute time, questioning, investigation, and experimentation.

The Ted Lasso analogy is intentional: this is one squad with three tiers of resource allocation, not three independent portfolios. A team is the resource envelope surrounding its players.

Players can move between teams as their observed performance changes.

## 2. The core principle

A team's identity is defined by the resources allocated to its players.

Those resources include:

- Invested capital.
- Analytical attention.
- Compute time and compute power.
- Depth and number of questions.
- Investigation and research effort.
- Time available for discussion, experimentation, and "training."

Therefore, team membership is not merely a label.

**Team assignment determines the level of resources invested in the player.**

## 3. The 35-player roster and fixed team sizes

The planning roster contains 35 players. Team sizes remain fixed:

| Team | Number of players | Invested-capital target per player | Relative attention |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Team A | 10 | $20 | 4 |
| Team B | 10 | $10 | 2 |
| Team C | 15 | $5 | 1 |
| **Total** | **35** | — | — |

At full target allocation, the conceptual capital targets are:

- Team A: $200
- Team B: $100
- Team C: $75
- Total: $375

These are target levels for the conceptual model. They are not a claim that every player currently has that amount invested, nor are they a statement of current market value.

The three capital tiers remain distinct: Team A targets $20, Team B targets $10, and Team C targets $5.

## 4. What each team means

### Team A — Highest resource allocation

- 10 players.
- Approximately $20 invested-capital target per player.
- Highest analytical attention.
- Highest compute allocation.
- More questions and deeper investigation.
- More time for discussion, experimentation, and risk analysis.

Team A is the squad's highest-priority resource envelope.

### Team B — Intermediate resource allocation

- 10 players.
- Approximately $10 invested-capital target per player.
- Intermediate analytical attention and compute.
- Meaningful investigation and discussion, but less than Team A.

### Team C — Exploratory resource allocation

- 15 players.
- Approximately $5 invested-capital target per player.
- Lowest defined resource allocation.
- Concise but still meaningful review.
- Players can earn substantially more resources through performance.

Team C is not a discard pile. It is a lower-resource development environment.

## 5. Performance is the primary movement mechanism

Promotion and demotion are **performance-driven, not information-driven**.

Information is used to understand, evaluate, and explain performance. It does not directly promote or demote a player.

Performance is evaluated relative to the player's relevant category and expectations. Players should not be compared indiscriminately across unrelated categories.

### Rolling three-checkpoint window

Promotion and demotion are evaluated using the player's **most recent three meetings/checkpoints**.

At each checkpoint, the system asks:

1. How did the player perform over the latest three checkpoints?
2. How does that performance compare with the expectations of its category?
3. Does the evidence justify keeping the player in its current team, promoting it, or demoting it?

A player that materially underperforms across the latest three checkpoints can be demoted.

A player that materially outperforms the expectations of its category across the latest three checkpoints can be promoted.

This is a rolling window. After each new checkpoint, the oldest observation drops out and the newest observation enters.

### Direct movement

Moves can be direct between any tiers.

A player may move:

- Team C → Team B
- Team B → Team A
- Team C → Team A
- Team A → Team B
- Team B → Team C
- Team A → Team C

Passing through Team B is not mandatory.

## 6. Promotion and demotion are all-or-nothing resource changes

A promotion is not merely a change of label.

**When a player is promoted, the resource allocation moves with the player.**

For example, Team B → Team A means the player receives:

- The higher Team A capital target.
- The higher Team A analytical-attention allocation.
- The higher Team A compute-time allocation.
- More questions and deeper investigation.
- More discussion and experimentation capacity.

Likewise, demotion moves the player into the lower resource envelope.

The purpose of this mechanism is not merely to reward performance. It creates a feedback loop:

**Better performance → more resources → deeper investigation → faster detection of opportunities and risks.**

This is especially important when performance and information disagree.

A high-performing player with emerging risk signals can therefore be promoted precisely because the higher resource allocation allows the system to investigate the risk sooner and more deeply.

## 7. Fixed team capacity

Team sizes cannot expand or contract:

- Team A: 10
- Team B: 10
- Team C: 15

If a promotion enters a full destination team, the lowest-performing player in the receiving team must leave that team to create room.

The displaced player is then assessed for the appropriate lower tier. If a chain of movements is required, the model must preserve the fixed 10 / 10 / 15 roster sizes.

This is a classification and resource-allocation rule, not proof that any trade occurred.

## 8. Analytical attention and compute allocation

The agreed illustrative resource ratio is:

**Team A : Team B : Team C = 4 : 2 : 1**

This ratio applies to limited analytical resources, including model attention and compute time.

For example, if a review has a 3,500-token analytical budget:

| Team | Illustrative budget | Relative allocation |
|---|---:|---:|
| Team A | 2,000 tokens | 4 |
| Team B | 1,000 tokens | 2 |
| Team C | 500 tokens | 1 |

These are ceilings for the exercise, not mandatory word counts. The allocation should be used for meaningful analysis rather than padding.

The resource allocation is not itself an investment rating. More compute means the system can investigate more deeply; it does not automatically mean the player should receive more capital.

## 9. Six-hour checkpoint cadence

Execution and investment evaluation occur at checkpoints every 6 hours.

Across a standard three-day cycle there are 12 checkpoints.

At each checkpoint, the system attempts to obtain fresh information.

The previous rule requiring a 24-hour freeze on external information is superseded.

### Fresh-information rule

At every checkpoint:

1. Attempt to obtain new, verified information.
2. If material new information exists, incorporate it.
3. If no genuinely new information is available, expand, deepen, or reinterpret the best verified information already available.
4. Never fabricate an update merely to make a checkpoint appear new.
5. Do not mechanically repeat the previous checkpoint when additional analytical depth can be obtained.

Therefore:

**The cadence is six hours for evaluation and information gathering, not six hours of forced repetition.**

The team repeatedly re-engages with reality.

## 10. Performance and information are separate dimensions

The model maintains two distinct streams of evidence.

### Performance

Performance is observable and is the primary trigger for team movement.

Examples include relevant price movement and other measurable results appropriate to the player's category.

### Information

Information includes news, fundamentals, protocol developments, macro conditions, sentiment, liquidity, institutional activity, regulatory developments, and other evidence relevant to the player.

Information helps explain performance, identify risks, and determine investment action.

**Information does not replace performance as the promotion/demotion mechanism.**

A player can therefore have:

- Strong performance + positive information.
- Strong performance + negative information.
- Weak performance + positive information.
- Weak performance + negative information.

The system must preserve those distinctions rather than collapse them into one sentiment score.

## 11. Investment decision is separate from team movement

Promotion/demotion and investment action are related but distinct decisions.

**Performance determines the player's team/resource allocation.**

**Performance + current information + risk analysis determine the investment action.**

Investment actions may include:

- Buy / increase exposure.
- Hold.
- Reduce / harvest.
- Exit.
- Investigate before taking the next capital action.

However, a promotion itself is an immediate resource-allocation decision. It means more capital and more analytical/compute resources are allocated to the player.

### Example: Ethereum

Consider an illustrative Team B player, Ethereum.

Meeting 12:
- Price: $120.
- Information: bullish buying.

Meeting 13:
- Price: $200.
- Information: increasing liquidity and significant bank investment.

Meeting 14:
- Price: $250.
- Information: sentiment has turned bearish because participants fear Ethereum is becoming a bubble.

The complete three-meeting state is therefore:

**Performance:** $120 → $200 → $250 = strong upward performance.

**Information:** bullish → stronger bullish evidence → bearish/bubble concern.

The correct model response is:

**PROMOTE Ethereum from Team B to Team A.**

Because the rolling three-checkpoint performance justifies promotion.

The promotion is all-or-nothing:

- Increase capital allocation to the Team A target.
- Increase analytical attention.
- Increase compute time and compute power.
- Increase depth of investigation.

At the same time, the bearish information remains an explicit risk signal.

The investment analysis therefore does not blindly interpret promotion as “the asset is safe.” The higher Team A resource allocation is precisely what allows the system to investigate the bubble concern sooner and more deeply.

In this example, the decision is:

**PROMOTE + INVEST + INVESTIGATE**

The system does not postpone the Team A resource allocation merely because the latest information is concerning.

## 12. Resource feedback loop

The intended operating loop is:

**OBSERVE → MEASURE PERFORMANCE → CLASSIFY → ALLOCATE RESOURCES → INVESTIGATE → ACT → OBSERVE AGAIN**

A player's performance can earn more resources.

More resources provide more analytical and computational capacity.

More analytical capacity can identify opportunities and risks sooner.

The next observed performance then determines whether the player continues to justify its current team.

This creates a dynamic system rather than a static portfolio classification.

## 13. Checkpoint decision process

At every 6-hour checkpoint:

1. Gather the latest verified performance data.
2. Gather fresh verified information where available.
3. If no new information exists, deepen the existing evidence rather than fabricate novelty.
4. Evaluate the player's rolling three-checkpoint performance.
5. Compare performance with the player's category expectations.
6. Determine whether promotion, demotion, or no movement is justified.
7. Apply fixed team capacity if a destination team is full.
8. Apply the corresponding all-or-nothing resource allocation for the new team.
9. Evaluate current investment action using performance, information, and risk.
10. Record proposed allocation separately from actual execution.
11. Preserve the project's evidence hierarchy.

## 14. Evidence hierarchy and execution discipline

This remains a planning model.

The project's canonical evidence hierarchy is:

**REAL EXECUTION → SOURCE EVENT LOG → DERIVED POSITION → STRATEGY LOT → DASHBOARD**

- An actual broker or exchange receipt is the source of truth for an execution.
- Never invent fills, prices, timestamps, or market data.
- Distinguish verified execution data from workbook values, calculations, assumptions, and analysis.
- Binance live quotes and execution receipts take precedence over stale workbook prices for crypto when making current execution assessments.
- Proposed capital allocations are not executed trades.
- A conceptual promotion does not prove that a trade occurred.
- If implemented in the workbook, verify formulas and ranges after structural changes.
- Read the Dashboard last because it is derived from upstream data.

## 15. What this draft deliberately does not define

This draft does not yet impose:

- A universal numerical performance score.
- A hard percentage threshold for promotion.
- A hard percentage threshold for demotion.
- A universal formula for comparing categories.
- A permanent assignment of all 35 players.
- A fixed number of checkpoints required beyond the rolling three-checkpoint window.
- A guarantee that every promotion results in an immediate market order.

Those mechanisms should be explicitly adopted before being treated as project rules.

## 16. Summary

Qimono Trading is one 35-player squad divided into three fixed-size resource-allocation teams.

Team A: 10 players, $20 target, 4x analytical/compute allocation.

Team B: 10 players, $10 target, 2x analytical/compute allocation.

Team C: 15 players, $5 target, 1x analytical/compute allocation.

Team membership determines the player's complete resource envelope: capital, analytical attention, compute power, questioning, investigation, and discussion time.

Performance, measured through a rolling three-checkpoint window and evaluated against category expectations, is the primary mechanism for promotion and demotion.

Information is a separate evidence stream used to explain performance, identify risks, and determine investment action.

At every six-hour checkpoint, the system attempts to obtain fresh information. If none exists, it deepens the verified information already available rather than fabricating novelty.

Promotion and demotion are all-or-nothing resource changes.

The operating philosophy is:

**Performance earns resources.  
Information guides investigation.  
Resources create better investigation.  
Better investigation improves the next decision.**

All proposed allocations remain conceptual until supported by verified execution data.
